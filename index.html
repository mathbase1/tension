Understand the assembly

Two members, one jointM1 and M2 connect at J. The applied load is balanced by tension in these two members. All cases use the same two-member equilibrium content as the original worksheet.
Fixed locations are not fixed-end jointsThe hatched slab, wall or floor is stationary. Bolted clevis plates secure anchors A and B to it. The member ends are ideal pins: their locations cannot move, but they can rotate. No end moment is included.
The datum is not a third memberEach angle is measured between its member and the dashed reference line. The applied load acts in the opposite direction to that line.
Use an idealised, two-dimensional modelThe members are straight, their self-weight is neglected, the pins are frictionless and the load acts in the plane of the diagram. Both member forces are tensile in every generated case. Any hanging payload is represented by its total weight at J.
What the drawing does and does not showThe angles agree with the generated geometry. Member lengths and hardware sizes are illustrative, not dimensions to measure. The force overlay retains the installed supports and adds outward tension arrows; it is not a separate free-body diagram.
Practice marking, not a design toleranceThe calculated answer is rounded to two decimal places. Answers in 0.01 kN steps from 1.00 kN below to 1.00 kN above that value are accepted, including both endpoints. This educational tolerance is not a safe working load or a construction/design tolerance.
